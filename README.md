# Kiln Tile Bench

Inspect fired ceramic tiles from registered face, macro, or grazing-light photographs. The demonstration uses a synthetic indigo-border tile with deliberate pits, reflectance witnesses, missing camera support, and registration marks.

Run `./start.sh` and open the local address it prints. The default port is 19991. Compose stores all runs and archived evidence in the named tile volume. Run `./start.sh --down` to stop the app and keep its data.

Upload JPEG, PNG, or TIFF photographs and click the tile's four known corners clockwise from upper left. Use paired photographs of the same measured extent for shared observation or color comparisons. Mark a patch, a two-point rim transect, or ordered decal landmarks, then choose **Take reading**. A saved reading retains each original photograph, its calibration, the marks, measured values, and the annotated overlay. Download a ZIP or CSV from a saved reading. The Image preparation room provides independent crop, color, and image comparison tools.

Measurements include glaze tone, glaze and decal contrast, decal likeness area, brush spacing, compact dark pinholes, the glaze rim transition, decal offsets, grazing response against marked witnesses, gloss scale checks, paired visibility, raking spots through an adopted scale, and fired color difference. Use a closely controlled photograph for each comparison. Pinhole detection separates compact dark candidates from broad marks using a luminance threshold and physical size. Grazing response measures the difference between two photographs against their own matte and gloss witnesses. These are image measurements, with no calibration to physical gloss units or ceramic material identification.

A **Gloss scale check** marks two independent matte/gloss witness pairs plus one ordinary-glaze check patch per scale on the same two photographs. Each check patch is read as its difference from the scale's own matte witness, divided by that scale's matte-to-gloss separation, so the two readings are comparable even when the witnesses differ in strength. Enter the expected check value and the interval it must fall in; a scale whose check patch falls outside the interval is reported but cannot be adopted. When both scales stay inside, the reading is chosen by the selection rule: **Wider matte–gloss separation** prefers the scale whose two witnesses are furthest apart (steadier against small fluctuations), while **Reading closest to the expected value** prefers the smaller deviation, breaking a near tie with the wider separation. Adopting a check stores the chosen scale together with both source photographs, their calibration, the three patch marks, the witness values and the interval. Adopted scales are never rewritten.

Grazing response and Raking spot map can be read through an adopted scale. Those readings are frozen to that scale's own two photographs (matte and gloss marks reused as-is, any recalibration or mark deletion ignored) and record the scale reference alongside their results. A new Grazing response can still be read independently without touching the adopted references. A Raking spot map through a scale finds compact highlights in the grazing photograph that stand out against the diffuse photograph in units of the adopted matte-to-gloss separation, and lists positions that only one of the two photographs resolves as **Missing evidence** instead of guessing.

A run accepts at most 12 photographs, 100 marks, 160 readings, and 24 adopted gloss scales. Photograph input is limited to 20 MiB and 14 megapixels. Calibrated output uses up to four pixels per millimetre and a long edge at most 1,300 pixels. Transparent and fully clipped positions are left unmeasured.
